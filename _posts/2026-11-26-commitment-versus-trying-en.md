How often do you hear yourself saying, "Okay, I'll try my best"?

It sounds humble, cooperative, and polite. But in professional coaching, we often notice something quiet underneath: "I'll try" is often an unspoken insurance policy we buy to protect ourselves from failure before we even start.

When we say "I'll try," our mind is usually keeping one foot out the door, ready to walk away if things get tough.

<!--more-->

### The Core Idea: Trying Leaves Room for Failure; Commitment Anchors the Outcome

> **Key Takeaway:**  
> **Trying** is a defensive stance where we leave our success to luck:  
> *Trying + Doubt = Pre-planned Failure.*  
> **Commitment** is an inner choice where we own the finish line, even when the path is uncertain:  
> *Commitment + Action = Real Results.*

Many people fear making a true commitment because they confuse it with having to do everything alone.

In coaching, two startup founders, Amy and George, were great at client sales and team building, but they stalled on building their company website for four whole months. At every weekly review, they would sigh and say, "We'll try to find time this weekend to get the website done." Four months went by, and nothing changed. Both felt drained and frustrated.

During a coaching session, the coach asked a simple question:  
"Are you committed to making your business a success, or are you committed to coding a website yourself?"

They both went quiet. They realized neither of them liked coding or web design. They had felt guilty, thinking that "good founders must do it all." Once they saw that their real commitment was simply to have a professional website for their customers—not to suffer alone over code—the tension lifted. That same afternoon, they asked a creative teammate to help. Within two weeks, the website was live.

True commitment never means suffering in silence. It means knowing what outcome truly matters and finding the wisdom to reach it.

### Mindset Comparison Card: Trying vs. Commitment

Notice how our body and words reveal our mindset:

> 🌲 **Mindset Comparison: Trying vs. Commitment**
>
> ❌ **The Trying Stance (Waiting to Back Out)**
> ・Typical Words: "I'll try," "We'll see how it goes," "If I have time."
> ・Focus: Worrying about obstacles, preparing excuses in advance, fearing judgment.
> ・Inner Feeling: Anxious, tired, stuck, drained of energy.
> ・Usual Outcome: Giving up easily at the first bump, blaming outside circumstances.
>
> 💡 **The Commitment Stance (Focused on the Outcome)**
> ・Typical Words: "I commit to getting this done by Friday," "If this way fails, I'll try another path."
> ・Focus: Keeping eyes on the goal, treating obstacles simply as road conditions.
> ・Inner Feeling: Calm, curious, proactive, open to asking for help.
> ・Usual Outcome: Pulling together resources, learning quickly, moving steadily forward.

### Mind Tea Journal · Coaching Questions

Pour yourself a cup of warm tea and take two minutes to reflect quietly:

> 🌿 **Coaching Reflection Questions:**
>
> 1. Think about something you have put off for weeks. Are you secretly in "trying mode" or "commitment mode"?
> 2. If you change "I'll try" to "I commit to...", what changes in your chest and shoulders?
> 3. Have you mistaken "reaching the goal" with "having to do it all by myself"? Who could be a good partner to help?

### A Micro-Step Experiment for Today

You do not need a big speech to change an old habit. Try this small experiment today:

* **Step 1: Catch Your "I'll Try" Reflex**  
  Whenever you catch yourself about to say "I'll try," pause for two seconds. Notice: what small fear is hiding there?
* **Step 2: Name the True Outcome**  
  Ask yourself: "What is the single most important result I actually want to create here?"
* **Step 3: Ask for One Small Piece of Help**  
  If one part of the task drains your energy, reach out and ask a colleague or friend for support today.
