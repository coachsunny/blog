# Letting Go of Unrealistic Goals Isn't Quitting: Fueling and Flexibly Maintaining Change (Part 2)

Many of us grew up believing that true self-discipline means gritting our teeth and never backing down, no matter what.

When we fall behind or realize our plan no longer fits real life, a heavy wave of guilt washes over us: *"I failed again. I always give up halfway."*

In coaching, we look at this from a very different angle:

**Holding onto a goal that no longer works isn't discipline—it's mental rigidity.**

Real "maintenance" is never about sticking to a rigid wooden target. It is about keeping your curiosity, energy, and hope alive as you move forward.

In [Part 1](/post/commitment-to-change-navigating-barriers-part-1/), we explored the three starter switches of change: taking **Ownership**, giving yourself the right amount of **Time**, and preparing for **Difficulty** with practical safety nets before leaving the harbor.

Now, as your ship enters open waters, the real voyage begins. You face mental fatigue, the fog of daily distractions, and sudden changes in your schedule.

To keep moving forward without burning out, you need three sustaining gears.

---

## Gear 4: Notice Immediate "Rewards"—Your Emotional Fuel

Building a new habit is not just a test of character; it is a rewiring of your brain's neural pathways.

Our brains run on a simple rule: if an action brings no positive feeling, the brain marks it as painful and naturally begins to procrastinate. Genuine, good emotions are the fuel that keeps you going.

Yet, most people get trapped in what psychologists call **"The Gap"**:
They compare where they are today—still clumsy, still making mistakes—with an idealized, flawless version of themselves far in the future. The result? Exhaustion and defeat.

To break free, we must shift to **"The Gain"**—measuring backward from where you started rather than measuring forward against perfection:

> 🌲 **Mental Framework: Two Ways to Look at Progress**
>
> ❌ **The Gap Mindset (Running Out of Energy)**
> ・Focus: Comparing today's messy effort against an impossible perfect goal.
> ・Result: Feeling behind, anxious, and beating yourself up.
>
> 💡 **The Gain Mindset (Building Real Confidence)**
> ・Focus: Comparing today with where you started before you made this choice.
> ・Result: Real pride in small steps, giving your mind steady fuel.

In coaching sessions, clients often tell me: *"Sunny, I messed up again this week. I lost my patience with my team."*

I often remind them:
**"Even if you only realize it after it happened, the fact that you noticed your old reaction is already huge progress."**

In the past, you were running on autopilot. Today, you are aware. Awareness itself is the beginning of transformation, and that awareness deserves to be acknowledged.

---

## Gear 5: Cut Through "Distractions"—Let Vision Guide Your Attention

In our hyper-connected world, what stops us is rarely a giant crisis. More often, it is a thousand small interruptions: notifications, feeds, and daily chores.

Many people try to fight distraction with strict force: deleting apps, locking their phones away, or gritting their teeth. But psychology shows that whatever you fight with pure resistance often pushes back twice as hard.

The secret is not fighting the noise; it is **reconnecting with the deeper vision you set during Step 1 (Ownership)**.

> 🌲 **Mental Framework: Two Ways to Handle Distractions**
>
> ❌ **Pure Willpower Battle**: Trying to force yourself to ignore all pings, ending in burnout.  
> 💡 **Reconnecting with Vision**: Creating a 10-minute quiet island each day to grow steadily.

When your inner vision is clear, small distractions naturally fade into the background. You don't need a month off from life to grow. Instead, you develop the ability to find quiet pockets inside a busy day.

Five minutes of calm reflection on the train, taking three deep breaths between meetings, or listening deeply to a loved one—these small islands of focus build real resilience.

---

## Gear 6: Flexible "Maintenance"—Redefining True Responsibility

This is the mindset shift that brings the most relief.

Many people treat their goals like a rigid machine: once set on January 1st, it must run without pause for 365 days.

Real life, however, flows unpredictably. Careers shift, family needs arise, and our physical energy rises and falls. When your circumstances change dramatically, a goal you wrote months ago may no longer make sense.

Pushing through blindly often leads to burnout and self-blame.

Remember this:
**When your life changes, pausing to review your path and adjusting your goal is not giving up. It is the highest form of self-responsibility.**

> 🌲 **Mental Framework: Adjusting When Life Changes**
>
> ❌ **Rigid Targets**: Forcing yourself through illness or chaos, feeling like a failure.  
> 💡 **Flexible Targets**: Honestly adjusting your daily steps to protect your confidence.

Replacing a goal that suffocates you rescues you from the trap of feeling like a failure.

It frees your energy from self-criticism and helps you ask the only question that matters right now: **"Given my current energy, what is one kind, honest, and doable step I can take today?"**

---

> 🌿 **Mind Tea Journal · Coaching Inquiries:**
>
> 1. **Looking at Progress**: Think of a recent attempt where you felt you "messed up." If you look back at who you were a year ago, what small, real capability has quietly taken root in you?
> 2. **Lighthouse Metaphor**: If your goal is a lighthouse to guide you rather than a tool to judge you—given the current winds in your life, what kind of warmer, more grounded starting point would you like to set for yourself today?

---

## 🍵 Micro-Step Experiment: Three Practices for Sustainable Momentum

Sustainable change does not require dramatic gestures. It only takes three gentle adjustments today:

* **Step 1: Notice the Gain (Reward)**:
  Tonight before sleeping, write down one moment where you caught an old habit in action. Instead of judging yourself, say gently: *"I noticed it. That is real progress."*
* **Step 2: Micro-Island of Focus (Distraction)**:
  Amid daily interruptions, don't try to disappear for hours. Carve out a sacred **10-minute Focus Island**—turn off notifications and spend ten minutes on one thing that serves your deeper vision.
* **Step 3: Reset the Target (Maintenance)**:
  Look honestly at any goal that currently makes you feel trapped or anxious. If it no longer fits your life, take a deep breath and cross it out. Replace it with a step so small and compassionate that it feels natural to take.
